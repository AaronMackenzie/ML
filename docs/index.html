Linear regression

Fit a straight line to city population versus food-truck profit. Click the chart to add a point and watch the least-squares line, R² and error move.

What it does

Finds the line y = m·x + c that minimises the sum of squared vertical distances to the data.

How this repo does it

LinearRegression.py uses the closed form: m = (mean(xy) − mean(x)·mean(y)) / (mean(x²) − mean(x)²). The page uses the same formula.

Reading R²

R² is the share of the variance in profit that the line explains. 1 is a perfect fit; 0 means no better than the average.

02 · Supervised · Classification

Logistic regression

Predict whether a student is admitted from two exam scores. Press Train and watch gradient descent bend the probability field into a decision boundary.

Try a learning rate near 1.0: it learns fast but the cost can wobble. At 0.001 it barely moves.

What it does

Passes a weighted sum of the inputs through the sigmoid function to get a probability between 0 and 1, then thresholds at 0.5.

A fix found while building this

The original script trained on raw scores (0–100) and the sigmoid overflowed, giving 60% accuracy. Standardising the inputs first lifts it to 89%, as in this demo.

The boundary

The line where θ₀ + θ₁·Exam1 + θ₂·Exam2 = 0. Everything on one side is predicted admitted.

What it does

Repeats two moves: give each point to its nearest centroid, then move each centroid to the mean of its points. Stops when nothing moves.

Why random starts matter

Different starting centroids can settle on different clusters. Use Random start a few times to see it.

Matches the script

Iteration 1 gives centroids (5.171, 3.171), (5.5, 4.2), (6.45, 2.95) with shift 1.5886, exactly what Kmeans.py prints.

Principal component analysis

The iris dataset has four measurements per flower. PCA squeezes them into two axes that keep as much of the spread as possible.

What it does

Builds the covariance matrix, finds its eigenvectors, and projects each flower onto the two with the largest eigenvalues.

Reading the plot

Species 1 separates cleanly. Species 2 and 3 overlap a little, so two axes do not tell the whole story.

Small difference

The script projects the raw values; this page subtracts the mean first. The picture is the same, just centred on zero.
